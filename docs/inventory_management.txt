Inventory Management Fundamentals

Safety stock is buffer inventory held above expected demand to protect against variability in demand or lead time. It is typically sized using a service-level target and the standard deviation of demand during lead time: safety_stock = z * sigma_demand * sqrt(lead_time). Higher service levels require a higher z-score and therefore more buffer stock, which trades off against holding cost.

The reorder point (ROP) is the inventory level that triggers a new purchase order. It is calculated as ROP = average_daily_demand * lead_time_days + safety_stock. If on-hand inventory falls below the reorder point, a replenishment order should be placed to avoid a stockout before the new stock arrives.

Economic order quantity (EOQ) is the order size that minimizes the sum of ordering cost and holding cost. It is calculated as EOQ = sqrt((2 * annual_demand * order_cost) / holding_cost_per_unit). Ordering too frequently in small batches drives up ordering cost; ordering too infrequently in large batches drives up holding cost and ties up working capital.

ABC analysis segments SKUs by contribution to revenue or usage value. "A" items are typically the top 10-20% of SKUs by value and account for 70-80% of total value; they warrant tighter inventory control and more frequent review. "C" items are numerous but low-value and are usually managed with simple, low-touch replenishment rules to avoid wasting planning effort.
